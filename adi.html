1 Disclaimer

This is a personal opinion. This is anonymous for many reasons. The perceptions are my honest experience. There is no intention to harm any individual. It is my sincere opinion that all individuals indirectly referred to are intelligent, well-intentioned moral people. This assumption does not affect my personal experience.

2 Preamble

I am a medical doctor1 close to retirement. I was formally diagnosed with Asperger’s syndrome (as was the label at the time) around 30 years ago. This diagnosis was made in the context of a depressive illness. I never disclosed, declared or thought about this label. I just carried on.

In retrospect, my daughter, as a teenager, had point blank told me, “but dad, you have Aspergers”. I didnit really think about it till the diagnosis.

My interest in this label changed after watching my grandchildren exhibiting what now might be called neurodiergent features. Some of these traits resonated with my childhood experience. I reflected on this and confronated the prospect of my grandchildren experiencing isolation and challenges and obstacles that would interfere with them reaching their potential. This “imagined” forecast was a result of reflection on how features of my own neurodivergnce contributed to my own difficulties, setbacks and perceived unfulfilled ambitions. Of course, this was entirely speculative and as my wife correctly observed: “projection of my own insecurities on to my grandchildren”.

I misguidedly wrote to people who were academic experts and researchers to identify resources amd guidance. I never receievd a reply.

I came across Autistic Doctors International during my dearching. I still remain uncertain exactly why I joined. Perhaps, it was looking for a tribe, perhaps it was trying to understand myself. I really don’t know.

3 Autistic Doctors International

3.1 Joining

There was an appropriate screening process and helpful guidance to join the private forum and suggested WhatsApp groups.

I have never been part of an online community and I had and have no real frame of reference.

I certainly absolutely respect the confidentiality and anonymity of the members.

3.2 Impressions

3.2.1 Aims of the groups

I think the aims of support, advocacy, research (social scientific), inclusion and tolarance are worthy aims.

I believe the leadership and general members have this as a common belief and in general support members and have an important advocacy role.

3.2.2 General observations

The membership and leadership appear predominantly UK based with US next. This also applies to the leadership. There appears to be a femaile predominance. These are in now way judgements, just observations. In fact, the increased recognition of females with autism and a forum to discuss, share resources and support seems a noble enterprise.

3.2.3 My participation

I shared some of my experience and was grateful for supportive comments. I tried to provide supportive comments in both Facebook site and the WhatsApp groups I joined. My support and advice was non-specific, general and often saccharine, I was never sure it had any value at all. I also had the perception that I held either out of date or discordant viewpoints in relation to the majority of members. I did not know of any of the practical resources (as the majority of members were not whare U work). Frankly, I had no idea of any local resources myself.

4 Why I left the group

There were 3 principal reasons why I left the group:

  1. I dont think i brought value to the group
  2. The group provided me no value and potential harm
  3. Discordant views compared with the leadership and majority of members

4.1 My lack of value to group

There were many posts (Facebook and WhatsApp) that expressed similar experiences to mine. The majority of these were related to challenges, obstacles, burn-out and meltdowns. I thought, by virtue of survival there was some intrinsic value to my lived experience. However, in time, I came to appreciate that a lot of persistence and development related to serendipity and the unearned and unanticipated kindness of a small but significant group of people. Therefore, any advice I could proffer was confined to non-specific, generic encouragement.

I had no specific knowledge of resources or groups that could provide practical help.

I was also from a different era and ignorant of current thinking, therapies and accomodations. Some of my responses had the potential to be unintentionally provocative or triggering (the oppostite of my intention).

4.2 Group’s lack of value/potential harm to me

My passive and active interactions (related in part to some of the experiences mentioned in Section 4.1) were frequently triggering to me and this triggering started to adversely affect my health.

I left the WhatsApp groups. I sent an explanatory email to the administrators. I received no reply.

This lack of interest in one members experience, indirectly further contributed to a sense of not belonging or at least a sense of invisibilty. I have experienced this perception all my life. I thought it was part of the weird misfit trait of neurodivergence. I just accepted it. It was, however, painful and anomalous to experience this from a group specifically aimed at includion, tolerance and cekebrating diversity.

4.3 Discordance in philosophical viewpoint(s) with group

My awareness of this divergence was slow and incremental.

  1. Reading the comments from other uses including some intense and sometimes extremely perjorative views triggered by what seemed to me innocent or at worst misguided comments by other users or referring to opinion pieces from various sources (peer-reviewed and not)
  2. the aforementioned absolute silence in relation to my feedback regarding the WhatsApp groups.
  3. I emailed one of the leadership group about some of the limitations of one of the group’s descriptive social research. There was again no reply (see Figure 1)
  4. after I posted an interview with Uta Frith to promote general discussion I was shocked by the degree of franklu vitriolic commentary it elicited from some members. It was not ,y intention to provocative. The definitional issue seems to me central to increasing our understanding and guide interventions (be theu accomodations or otherwise) rationally. I had comtacted Professor Frith to understand her perspective. Professor Frith kindly replied and provided a reasonable response.
  5. an increasing unease regarding the liberation of the diagnosis and the emphasis of the gifts of the neurodivergent doctor. There were frequent, somewhat derisive, use of terms like “normies” or “NTs”. This appeared to be elitism and divisive rather than inclusive and tolerant. This was under the umbrella of neuroaffirmative viewpoint. This has not been my experience at all. I have found no value in the label. I have found potential and observed real harm in labels of all sorts (not just confined to the neurodivergent/ neurotypical dichotomization). I am not a savant. I have been an average doctor. However, there’ are aspects attributable to neurodivergence that are helpful in my job and aspects that are unhelpful
  6. Finally, when a lawsuit was made public between leadership and a former member, I felt focus on power and politics were on display and (from my solitary viewpoint) detracted from the purpose of the group.

I, therefore, made the decision to leave the group.

5 Conclusion

This is a personal viewpoint. The modern world is flooded with opinions and the access to unfiltered, unsubstantiatied and increasingly outrageous and fabricated (maliciously or unintentioal AI hallucinations presented as “fact”). I do not seek to add to the dross.

However. I was motivated to write this to process my experience.

I acknowledge:

  1. I am old and have almost certainly got outdated, irrelevant or potentially harmful understanding of autism spectrum disorder or neurodivergence (whatever term or label suits)
  2. I sincerely believe the creation of the ADI has value and it’s stated aims and its role in support, advocacy and influence has real potential to improve the quality of life of many people
  3. I have no issue with any of the individuals (members, administrators or leadership). Frankly, I do not know them. It takes me years to feel comfortable with any person and, Frankly, there are only are very small number of people who I see and who see me.
  4. I sincerely beleive that better research including defintional issues, genetics, neurobiology are needed to improve understanding. I hope understanding informs and improves quality of life of life of future generations including my grandchildren.
  5. I think there remains many challenges for autistic doctors and ongoing suffering and I sincerely applaud the efforts of the ADI. I also appreciated the need for ideological framing and zeal to achieve meaningful outcomes rather than mere expressions of good intentions.There is politics in everything. I abhor politics and perhaps this sensitivity contributed to my decision.

However, I have a formal diagnosis (albeit from an old paradigm and out-dated term), a lived experience as a medical practioner for over 40 years and have been committed to trying the best for the patients I serve and involved in medical education all this time. It was, therefore, unexpected that a group whose name and stated purpose ultimately made me feel more isolated. My lived experience was more a cautionary tale than valuable. The experience, rather than inclusive, was ‘us“ and “them” and rather than :open to all viewpoints”, it was open to viewpoints that align with the, however well-intentioned, views of the majority. Perhaps, this is a natural attribute of most, if not all, human collective activities. I reiterate: this is not a criticim of ADI or any individuals. It is merely ,y personal experience and perceptions.

I think it is true that: if you have met one autistic person, you have met one autistic person. It is most liekly that I am not a “club” person. I have a small number of people who are more precious than gold. These are relationships that have taken years to develop and have formed in the absence of labels on any party.

I sincerely wish the members of the ADI all the best in their noble, courageous and important effort to support and advocate for autistic doctors.
